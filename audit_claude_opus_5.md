## Executive Summary

The vault is a well-engineered, unusually well-documented implementation of the Flap VaultPortal V2 spec. **Spec compliance is essentially clean**: it inherits an unmodified `VaultBaseV2`, `receive()` costs a measured **1,147 gas** (0.11% of the 1M cap), every revert is a bilingual literal `require`, the commission formula matches Rule 001/002 exactly and is monotonically *decreasing* and capped at 10%, and upgrade authority is provably Guardian-only. The launching developer holds **zero** post-launch privileges. Of 45 spec checklist items, 40 PASS, 1 FAILs, 3 are WARNINGs, and the rest are legitimately N/A.

The material risk is not in spec conformance but in **value routing and failure semantics**:

1. **H-01** — the vault is the grid *creator* in the UGM, but has no `claimPayout` path. All primary seat-sale proceeds, the creator's majority share of Harberger tax, and unsold-seat yield are **permanently stranded**. Recoverable today only via a beacon upgrade; irreversible if `lockVaultUpgrades()` is ever called.
2. **H-02** — `StockBasket.deposit` mints the *full* share amount even when a stock pull is silently skipped, breaking the "one share = fixed value in" anchor and diluting all prior holders. The existing test suite asserts this behaviour as correct.
3. **H-03** — slippage protection is caller-supplied with no oracle, and `distributeUniform` (the only write method in the UI schema) applies one *absolute* floor across legs of different price and decimals, so the operationally viable setting is effectively `0`.

A cross-cutting theme underlies most findings: **the contract's resilience story depends on best-effort skips, but every skip is silent** — five `catch` blocks swallow errors with no event. Every high/medium finding above degrades invisibly in production.

> **Status:** H-01 and H-02 have since been fixed, along with M-01, M-05, L-02 and L-03. H-03 was reviewed and **accepted as a known risk** rather than fixed — keeper-gating is the control, and the residual exposure is documented in that finding. See *Remediation Status* below.

No Critical findings. No reentrancy path was found; the share-minting design is structurally immune to first-depositor/donation inflation; `_disableInitializers()` is correctly called; the `CreateGridParams` ABI matches the canonical `GridTypes` struct field-for-field.

### Post-audit change: Flap Trigger Service integration (Rule 008)

The vault now implements `ITriggerReceiver` and schedules its own distributions through the Flap Trigger Service, which **removes the Guardian liveness dependency** flagged in the Centralization Analysis: `scheduleDistribute()` is permissionless, so anyone can arm the schedule and the service's callback performs the distribution — no keeper ever has to be appointed.

Rule 008 conformance: callback validates `msg.sender == triggerService()` (chain-fixed, §1); binds and **consumes** the single outstanding `requestId` before any effect, rejecting unknown and replayed ids (§2); re-checks the time-gate and pending balance at execution time and emits `TriggerSkipped` instead of forcing a stale action (§3); is `nonReentrant` (§5). The re-arm is isolated in `try/catch` so a fee shortfall pauses rather than breaks the loop, and `clearPendingTrigger()` (Guardian-only) recovers a permanently stuck request.

**Gas budget (§4) — measured, and it constrains the product.** Against live PancakeSwap V3 pools a 3-leg distribute costs **1,286,275 gas**; the marginal cost is ~159k/leg on the mock router plus a ~66k/leg premium for real pools, i.e. **~225k per leg on top of ~690k fixed**. Against the service's hard 2,000,000 cap:

| Legs | Estimated real callback | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | ~1.59M | ✅ ~80% of budget |
| 5 | ~1.82M | ⚠️ ~91% — no margin |
| 6 | ~2.04M | ❌ exceeds |
| 7 | ~2.27M | ❌ exceeds |

`MAX_TRIGGER_STOCKS = 5` was therefore introduced and `scheduleDistribute` rejects vaults with more legs, so the service never burns a fee on a callback that cannot fit. **A 7-stock basket cannot use the triggered path** and must fall back to the keeper path (`distribute` / `distributeUniform`), which has no gas cap — which reinstates the Guardian dependency for those vaults. This is a product decision that needs an explicit answer, not a default.

**Basket cut to 4 legs.** `StockConfig` was reduced from 7 stocks to **SPCXB, NVDAB, AAPLB, SPYB** (equal 2,500 bps weights) so a distribute fits the callback budget with ~20% headroom. QQQB, TSLAB and XAUt were dropped. `DeployVaultFactory.s.sol` and `LaunchVaultTokenTestnet.s.sol` were updated to match (`STOCK0..STOCK3`).

### Post-audit change: Flap reviewer findings (report v8)

Both findings from Flap's own interaction-risk review were confirmed real and fixed; both are marked **TP** in that report.

**v8 Finding 1 — Guardian rescue mechanism incomplete (High, `SYS-REQ-RESCUE-MECHANISM`).** The vault had facet (a), Guardian-only emergency withdrawal, but not facet (b), a Guardian-controlled forward switch on `receive()`. Because the vault is a Flap token's fee `marketAddress` and receives tax continuously, during an incident the Guardian could only re-sweep each new inflow by hand. Added `autoForwardEnabled` + `forwardAddress` (packed into one slot, default **off**) and Guardian-only `setAutoForward(bool, address)`; `receive()` now redirects arriving BNB and returns early when armed. The forward is a low-level call with a bounded 60,000-gas stipend whose result is deliberately ignored, so a reverting or gas-hungry destination can never make the fee transfer itself revert — Rule 005 is preserved and the BNB stays recoverable. This also closes the Rule 009 auto-forward items the original audit had marked N/A under the proxy exception.

**v8 Finding 2 — `PancakeV3YieldAdapter` yield-token collision (Low).** Confirmed by inspection: `FlapYieldAdapter` carries `yieldTokenInUse` precisely so its whole-balance sweep is attributable to one grid, and `PancakeV3YieldAdapter.registerPosition` had no equivalent guard. Two positions with overlapping token sets could therefore cross-contaminate — one grid's uncollected non-yield residual swept into another grid as yield. Added the same `yieldTokenInUse` mapping, the `require(!yieldTokenInUse[yieldTokenSide], "yieldToken in use")` check on register, and the release in `withdrawPosition`. Regression-tested in `test/fork/PancakeV3Adapter.fork.t.sol`.

**EIP-170:** these additions pushed the implementation over the 24,576-byte runtime limit (peaked at 26,111). It was brought back to **24,273 bytes (303 free)** by factoring `vaultUISchema`'s repeated per-method allocation into a `_readMethod` helper — the allocation code, not the string literals, dominated — and by shortening `description()`, which Rule 001 deprecates anyway. The margin is thin: measure `forge build --sizes` before adding anything further.

**Test changes:** `StockBasket.t.sol:559` previously asserted the H-02 defect as correct behaviour and has been inverted; new regression tests cover the claim path, the interval accounting, the self-gates, and per-share backing across a skipped round. `MockUGM.claimPayout` was made faithful to the real UGM (reverts `"nothing"` on a zero balance) so the wrapped-claim path is genuinely exercised.

### V-01: `vaultUISchema()` omits the permissionless bootstrap functions

**Rule**: 001 (`vaultUISchema` completeness) · **Severity**: Medium · **Status**: Open
**File**: `src/vault/StockpileBasketVaultV2.sol:759-829` (schema), omitting `:354`, `:679`

**Description**: The schema declares exactly 5 methods (4 reads + `distributeUniform`). Two **permissionless, user-callable, state-changing** functions are undeclared:

```solidity
function setupMarket() external nonReentrant { ... }      // :354
function registerWithGrid() external nonReentrant { ... }  // :679
```

`setupMarket()` is mandatory — until it runs, `basket == address(0)`, `_distribute` reverts with `"Market not set up / 市场未建立"` (`:498`), and the vault is inert. The vault's own `description()` (`:742`) instructs the reader to call it, but a generic Flap UI driven by `vaultUISchema()` has no button for it. `registerWithGrid()` is likewise required before yield can reach the grid; until then `_feedGrid()` silently best-effort-skips (`:664-667`).

The array-input `distribute(uint256[],uint256)` (`:450`) is **defensibly** omitted — documented at `:445-446`, and the scalar schema vocabulary genuinely cannot express `uint256[]`. Read-only `stocksLength()`, `stockAt()`, `isMarketSetUp()` are also unlisted (Low).

**Impact**: A vault launched through Flap and rendered by the auto-generated UI cannot be bootstrapped from that UI; the launcher must fall back to a block explorer. No funds at risk (BNB accrues safely, Guardian can recover), but the entire purpose of `vaultUISchema` — "the UI can automatically generate a full interaction page for any vault type" (`VaultBaseV2.sol:22-26`) — is defeated for the two actions that matter most at launch.

```solidity
schema.methods[5].inputs   = new FieldDescriptor[](0);
schema.methods[5].outputs  = new FieldDescriptor[](0);

schema.methods[5].isWriteMethod = true;

schema.methods[6].name = "registerWithGrid";

    unicode"One-time, permissionless: bind this vault as the UGM adapter for its grid so distribute() can deliver basket shares."
    unicode" / 一次性、无需许可：将本金库绑定为其网格的 UGM 适配器，使 distribute() 能够投递篮子份额。";
schema.methods[6].inputs   = new FieldDescriptor[](0);
```

### V-02: `minOutPerLeg` hard-codes `decimals = 18` for a heterogeneous stock amount

**Rule**: 001 (`vaultUISchema` field metadata) · **Severity**: Low · **Status**: Open
**File**: `src/vault/StockpileBasketVaultV2.sol:822-823`

**Description**: Per `IVaultSchemasV1.sol:183-187`, the UI multiplies the typed value by `10^decimals` before encoding. `minOutPerLeg` is applied to *every* stock leg (`:472-476`), and the basket's stocks are launcher-chosen arbitrary BEP-20s whose decimals may differ from 18 and from each other. A single `decimals` value cannot be correct for a heterogeneous set.

**Impact**: UI-only mis-scaling. A user typing "1" for an 8-decimal stock gets a floor 10^10× too high → every leg reverts inside the `try` (`:598`) → the round distributes nothing **and burns the `minInterval` window** (compounds M-01). No fund loss.

**Recommendation**: Set `decimals` to `0` and state that the value is a raw base-unit amount:

```solidity
schema.methods[4].inputs[0] = FieldDescriptor(
    "minOutPerLeg",
    "uint256",
    unicode"Uniform minimum output, in each stock's RAW base units (not decimal-adjusted), applied to every leg as the slippage floor. Use 0 to accept any non-zero output."
    unicode" / 统一的最小输出，以每种股票的原始最小单位计（未按小数位换算），作为每个腿的滑点下限。填 0 表示接受任何非零输出。",
    0
);
```

### H-01: All grid-creator proceeds are permanently stranded — the vault has no `claimPayout` path

**Severity**: High · **Status**: ✅ Fixed
**File**: `src/vault/StockpileBasketVaultV2.sol:398`; sink at `src/core/UnifiedGridManager.sol:333`, `:385`, `:446`, `:705`, `:725`; claim at `:581`

**Description**: `setupMarket()` calls `IUGM(ugm).createGrid(p)` directly (`:398`), so `_configs[gridId].creator == address(vault)` (`UnifiedGridManager.sol:238`) and all seats mint to the vault (`:247-258`). The UGM is fully pull-based: the creator's share of primary seat sales (`:333`), buyout creator fees (`:383`), Dutch clearing (`:446`), settled Harberger tax (`:705`), and all yield on creator-held-unsold or vacant seats (`:566`, `:576`, `:725`) are credited to `payouts[token][creator]` and withdrawable **only** by the creator calling `claimPayout(token)` (`:581-587`).

The vault has **no function that calls `claimPayout`**, and no generic execute/call escape hatch. Verified by grep across the whole repo: `claimPayout` appears only in the UGM and in tests — never in `src/vault/`. The local `IUGM` interface (`:39-47`) does not even declare it. `emergencyWithdrawToken` (`:717`) only moves tokens the vault already *holds*; the stranded value sits in the UGM's balance under the vault's `payouts` entry.

**Impact**: Using the factory schema's own advertised parameters (`gridSize` 100, `gridInitialPrice` 0.1 WBNB):

1. Launch → `setupMarket()` → grid created, creator = vault, 100 seats held by vault at 0.1 WBNB.
2. 100 users buy the primary listing → `_buyFromCreator` credits `payouts[WBNB][vault] += 0.1 - protocolFee` each.
3. **~10 WBNB is permanently locked.** Nobody — not users, not treasury, not the Guardian — can move it.
4. The same applies continuously to the creator share of settled Harberger tax (`:705` — the creator receives `10000 - protocolTaxBps` bps of *every* tax payment, i.e. the **majority of the grid's entire tax revenue**), and to basket shares landing on still-unsold/vacant seats (`:725`), pushed there by the vault's own `_feedGrid`.

Because the vault sits behind a BeaconProxy, the Guardian **can** recover this by upgrading the implementation (`Factory:168`) — the only reason this is High and not Critical. **If `lockVaultUpgrades()` (`Factory:173`) is ever called, the loss becomes irreversible and this becomes Critical.**

**Proof of Concept**:
```solidity

wbnb.approve(address(ugm), type(uint256).max);

// No selector on StockpileBasketVaultV2 calls ugm.claimPayout(...). Guardian's only lever:
vm.prank(guardian);
vault.emergencyWithdrawToken(address(wbnb), guardian);     // moves 0 — the WBNB is in the UGM
```

**Recommendation**: Add a **permissionless** sweep (permissionless, so recovery adds no further Guardian dependency):

```solidity
    require(basket != address(0), unicode"Market not set up / 市场未建立");
    try IUGM(ugm).claimPayout(token) returns (uint256 a) { amount = a; } catch {}
    emit GridPayoutClaimed(token, amount);
}
```

Add `function claimPayout(address) external returns (uint256);` to the local `IUGM` (`:39-47`). Define the destination policy explicitly: claimed **WBNB** needs no special handling (once on the vault it is counted by `gross` in the next `distribute`); claimed **basket shares** are re-fed through `_feedGrid`, which re-credits the vault's own unsold seats — each cycle moves the unsold-seat portion closer to sold seats, so it converges rather than looping, but it is worth documenting.

**Remediation applied.** `claimGridPayout(address token)` added to `StockpileBasketVaultV2`, permissionless and `nonReentrant`, gated on `basket != address(0)` and a non-zero token. The UGM call is wrapped in `try/catch` because the real `claimPayout` reverts `"nothing"` on a zero balance — a speculative call now returns 0 instead of reverting, so keepers and UIs can poll it safely. `claimPayout` was added to the local `IUGM`, and `GridPayoutClaimed(token, amount)` is emitted. `MockUGM.claimPayout` was corrected to revert `"nothing"` like the real UGM so the wrapped path is actually exercised rather than silently returning 0.

### H-02: `distribute` mints full-value shares even when a stock pull is skipped — dilutes existing holders and strands the stock

**Severity**: High · **Status**: ✅ Fixed
**File**: `src/vault/StockBasket.sol:160-170`, driven from `src/vault/StockpileBasketVaultV2.sol:637-650`

**Description**: `_depositToBasket` calls `StockBasket.deposit(amounts, spentWBNB, address(this))` (`:645`). Inside, each stock pull is wrapped in `try this._pull(...) catch { /* skip */ }` (`StockBasket.sol:161-165`), but the mint is **unconditional and always for the full amount**:

```solidity
if (amounts[i] > 0) {
    try this._pull(stocks[i], msg.sender, amounts[i]) {}
    catch { /* paused / blacklisting / reentrant stock: skip this leg */ }
}
...
_mint(to, sharesToMint);   // full amount regardless of how many legs actually landed
```

The share count is the contract's stated fairness anchor — "one share always represents the same amount of value put in" (`StockBasket.sol:20-25`). Skipping a pull breaks exactly that: supply grows by the full WBNB value while reserves grow by only part of it. The inline comment at `:156` acknowledges the behaviour ("its reserve simply stays lighter while shares still mint == `sharesToMint`") but does not account for the dilution it causes.

**Impact**: 7 legs, equal weights, `spentWBNB = 7 WBNB`. One leg's stock later blacklists the basket, is paused, or charges an FoT fee making the exact-`amounts[i]` `transferFrom` insufficient:

- Round 1 (healthy): supply 7e18, reserves ≈ 7 WBNB of stock. Per-share backing = 1.0.
- Round 2 (leg 4 reverts): `swapLeg` still succeeds (the pool trades fine), `amounts[3] > 0`, the pull is caught and skipped. Supply → 14e18; reserves grew by only ~6 WBNB.
- Per-share backing drops 1.0 → ~0.93. **Every seat holder from round 1 loses ~7% of their claim.**
- Leg 4's purchased stock now sits on the **vault**, not the basket, and is **never retried** — `_depositToBasket` only ever deposits *this round's* `amounts[i]`, so the residue is stranded until a Guardian sweep (see L-03).

Not hypothetical for this asset class: the "stock" tokens are tokenized-equity wrappers on BSC, which commonly implement pause and blacklist controls.

**Note**: the existing test at `test/vault/StockBasket.t.sol:575-579` **asserts the buggy behaviour** (`minted == 1000e18` while `bad.balanceOf(basket) == 0`) and must be inverted as part of the fix.

**Proof of Concept**:
```solidity
vault.distributeUniform(0, block.timestamp + 1);       // round 1: all 7 legs land
uint256 backing1 = basket.previewRedeem(1e18)[0];

badStock.setRevertTransferFrom(true);                  // pause / blacklist
vm.warp(block.timestamp + vault.minInterval());
vault.distributeUniform(0, block.timestamp + 1);       // round 2: leg 3 pull caught + skipped

assertEq(basket.totalSupply(), 14e18);                 // full mint anyway
assertGt(badStock.balanceOf(address(vault)), 0);       // stock stranded on the VAULT
assertLt(basket.previewRedeem(1e18)[0], backing1);     // prior holders diluted
```

**Recommendation**: Size the mint to the value that actually landed:

```solidity
// StockBasket.deposit — legSpend[] is the per-leg WBNB spend, supplied by the vault
uint256 landedWeight;
for (uint256 i = 0; i < amounts.length; i++) {
    if (amounts[i] == 0) continue;
    try this._pull(stocks[i], msg.sender, amounts[i]) { landedWeight += legSpend[i]; }
    catch { emit LegSkipped(i, stocks[i], amounts[i]); }
}
require(landedWeight > 0, unicode"No legs landed / 无成功的腿");
_mint(to, landedWeight);            // shares == value actually backed
```

This requires `_swapAll` (`StockpileBasketVaultV2.sol:576-607`) to return the per-leg `amountIn` array. Pair with L-03's fix (deposit balances rather than deltas) so a transient failure self-heals on the next round. At minimum, emit an event on every skipped pull — today the skip is completely silent.

**Remediation applied.** `StockBasket.deposit` now takes `(uint256[] amounts, uint256[] legValues, address to)` and accumulates `minted += legValues[i]` only inside the *success* branch of each pull, emitting `PullSkipped(i, stock, amount, legValue)` on failure. A round where nothing lands mints 0 and returns rather than reverting, so one universally-broken stock set cannot brick `distribute`. `_swapAll` now returns the per-leg WBNB spend alongside `amounts`, and `_depositToBasket` forwards it. L-03 was fixed in the same pass: `_depositToBasket` deposits each leg's full vault balance rather than this round's delta, so residue from a previously-skipped pull is swept in later instead of stranded — it enters unminted, accruing to all holders rather than being lost.

Covered by the inverted `test_Deposit_ReentrantStock_NeutralizedAndSkipped` and `test_Deposit_BlacklistStockSkipped_BestEffort` (both now assert `minted == 500e18`, the landed half, not the full round), plus the new `test_Deposit_SkippedLeg_DoesNotDilutePriorHolders` (asserts `totalSupply == total value landed` and that a prior holder's claim never shrinks) and `test_Deposit_NothingLanded_MintsZero`.

### H-03: Slippage protection is effectively non-binding — one scalar `minOut` across heterogeneous legs, silent skip on breach, plus keeper self-sandwich

**Severity**: High · **Status**: ⚠️ Accepted risk (see *Remediation decision* below) · *(also the Rule 003 fairness violation)*
**File**: `src/vault/StockpileBasketVaultV2.sol:466-478`, `:598-603`, `:613-632`

**Description**: Three properties compose into an exploitable MEV surface.

1. `distributeUniform(uint256 minOutPerLeg, uint256 deadline)` — the **only write method in `vaultUISchema`** (`:817`), therefore the one operators will actually use — applies *one absolute token amount* as the floor for every leg (`:474-476`). The legs buy **different tokens at different prices with different decimals**; a single absolute floor cannot bound all of them.
2. There is **no oracle and no expected-out computation anywhere**. `amountOutMinimum` comes entirely from the caller (`:626`).
3. If the floor *is* breached, `swapLeg` reverts inside `try` (`:598`) and the leg is **silently skipped** — no revert, no event. So an operator who sets a meaningful floor gets legs silently dropped; the operationally "safe" setting is `minOutPerLeg = 0` — which is exactly what the repo's own fork tests use.

**Impact**: Concrete sandwich, 2 legs, `net = 10 WBNB`, 50/50:

- Leg A buys token X at ~600 USDT → expected out ≈ 8.3e15.
- Leg B buys token Y at ~0.50 USDT → expected out ≈ 6e18, three orders of magnitude larger.
- To avoid silently dropping leg A, the uniform floor must be ≤ 8.3e15. Applied to leg B, that is a **99.86% slippage tolerance**.
- An MEV bot sees `distributeUniform(8.3e15, ...)` in the public BSC mempool, front-runs the USDT→Y hop ~50% (thin tokenized-equity pool), lets the vault buy at the inflated price, back-runs. The basket receives ~50% less Y than fair value while `_depositToBasket` mints the **full** `spentWBNB` shares (`:540`). The loss lands on seat holders' per-share backing.
- **Rule 003 dimension**: `onlyKeeper` (`:255-260`) includes any Guardian-appointed keeper, and the keeper *chooses* `minOut` and the execution moment. A keeper can set 0 and run the sandwich itself with no on-chain constraint. The NatSpec at `:207-208` claims keeper-gating "gates the slippage-bearing swap so an attacker cannot supply a zero floor" — true of external attackers only; it does nothing about the keeper, nor about mempool observers of the keeper's transaction.

**Mitigating**: `setKeeper` is `onlyGuardian` (`:687`) — the launching developer holds **no** privileges post-`initialize`, so there is no dev/insider role to abuse. Making `distribute` permissionless *as-is* would be strictly worse. Commission is capped at 10% and monotonically decreasing, so the fee path itself cannot be abused.

**Proof of Concept**:
```solidity
// 1. keeper broadcasts distributeUniform(minOutPerLeg = 8.3e15, deadline)
// 2. attacker front-runs in the same block:
router.exactInputSingle(USDT -> Y, 200_000e18);   // +50% on Y
// 3. vault leg B executes: amountOutMinimum = 8.3e15 << actual out (~3e18) => passes
// 4. attacker back-runs:
router.exactInputSingle(Y -> USDT, boughtY);      // realizes the spread
// Net: basket holds ~50% less Y; totalSupply still grew by the full 10e18.
```

**Recommendation**:
- **Derive `minOut` on-chain.** Read a TWAP via `IPancakeV3Pool.observe()` on both hops (or a Chainlink feed where available) and enforce `amountOutMinimum = expected * (10000 - maxSlippageBps) / 10000`, with `maxSlippageBps` a Guardian-only, capped parameter (≤300). Keep the caller value as an *additional* floor: `minOut = max(callerMinOut, oracleFloor)`.
- **Make a breach loud** — distinguish it from a dead-pool skip. Either fail closed (revert the whole `distribute`), or at minimum `emit LegSkipped(i, stock, amountIn, reason)` at `:601`.
- **Express `minOut` in bps of an oracle-derived expected out**, not an absolute token amount, so one scalar becomes dimensionally meaningful across heterogeneous legs.
- Once the floor is oracle-derived, `distribute` can safely become **permissionless**, removing the Guardian liveness dependency entirely (see Centralization Analysis).

**Remediation decision: accepted risk — keeper-gating is the control.** The project elected not to add an on-chain TWAP. The reasoning is defensible and is recorded here so a reviewer can weigh it rather than rediscover it:

- Only the Guardian can appoint a keeper (`setKeeper`, `:687`), and the Guardian is itself always a keeper. The launching developer holds no privileges at all after `initialize`, so there is **no insider role** that could abuse the floor — the trust assumption is exactly the Flap Guardian, which Rule 001 already makes unavoidable.
- Making `distribute` permissionless as-is would be strictly worse: any MEV bot could then pass `minOut = 0` itself.
- The genuine fix requires a manipulation-resistant TWAP, not a spot quoter (a quoter returns the very price a sandwicher moves). Tokenized-equity pools on BSC are thin and commonly sit at `observationCardinality == 1`, where `observe()` reverts — a naive TWAP floor would make those legs skip permanently, trading a value leak for a liveness failure.

The swap-authorization surface was re-verified as part of accepting this risk, since keeper-gating is now the *only* control: the router is reachable solely through `swapLeg` (`require(msg.sender == address(this))`) inside the private `_swapAll` → `_distribute`, whose two entrypoints are both `onlyKeeper`; the newly-added `approveStock` is self-gated identically; and the WBNB router allowance is reset to 0 at the end of every round, so no standing approval survives a distribute. Regression-tested by `testApproveStockOnlySelfReverts` and `testRouterAllowanceResetAfterDistribute`, alongside the existing `testSwapLegOnlySelfReverts` and `testDistributeRevertsForNonKeeper`.

**Residual risk, stated plainly:** a compromised or malicious Guardian-appointed keeper can still sandwich its own round and skim an unbounded fraction of that round's value, and a mempool observer can sandwich a keeper transaction that carries a loose floor. The exposure per incident is bounded by the BNB accrued since the last round; principal is never at risk. L-02's fix helps here — a breach now emits `LegSkipped`, so an under-spent round is at least observable off-chain. If third-party keepers are ever appointed, revisit this and implement the oracle floor.

**Update — the triggered path runs with a ZERO floor.** With the Flap Trigger Service integration, the primary distribution path is now the `trigger(uint256)` callback, which carries **no parameters at all** and therefore no `minOut`. The project's decision, after confirming with the Flap team, is to run those swaps with `amountOutMinimum = 0` and rely on the service submitting through a **private node RPC** so the execution transaction never reaches the public mempool. Two consequences a reviewer should weigh:

- Classic same-block sandwiching of the execution transaction is genuinely mitigated by the private relay.
- It is *not* eliminated as an economic risk. `FlapTriggerRequested` is a public event that announces `executeAfter` in advance, so an adversary can position around the known window without ever seeing the transaction. More importantly, a zero floor means **no protection at all against a badly-priced pool**, including the non-adversarial case of a pool drained or dislocated by unrelated activity — the vault will accept any output greater than zero.

This was raised, discussed, and consciously accepted in exchange for needing neither an off-chain keeper nor an on-chain oracle. A cheap partial mitigation remains available if it is ever revisited: a Guardian-set per-leg minimum price (`minOut = amountIn * floorRate`), which bounds catastrophic execution without requiring any TWAP machinery.

### M-01: A no-op or fully-failing `distribute` still burns the interval — up to 30 days of DoS

**Description**: `lastDistribute = block.timestamp` is written unconditionally *before* the swap phase (`:521`, justified as CEI) and is **never rolled back** when the round accomplishes nothing. The `spentWBNB == 0` branch (`:526-531`) returns 0 and emits, leaving `lastDistribute` advanced.

**Impact**: `minInterval` is launcher-chosen, capped at `MAX_MIN_INTERVAL = 30 days` (`:124`). ```

### M-03: `StockBasket.redeem` permanently forfeits a redeemer's slice of any stock whose transfer reverts

**Severity**: Medium · **Status**: Open
**File**: `src/vault/StockBasket.sol:222`, `:231-234`

**Description**: `redeem` burns the caller's shares at `:222` and *then* pays out each stock via `try this._payout(...) catch { amounts[i] = 0; }` (`:231-234`). A reverting leg is zeroed and the slice stays in the basket — but the shares are already burned, so the claim is gone forever.

**Impact**: A seat holder redeems 100 shares of a 7-stock basket while one stock is paused for 6 hours (routine for tokenized-equity wrappers around corporate actions), or blacklists the `to` address. The redeemer receives 6 of 7 slices; the 7th (~14% of their claim) is **permanently forfeited** and accrues to the *remaining* holders. No retry, no credit, no partial burn — a user who had waited 6 hours would have received it in full. Exploitable as a targeted grief: an attacker who can cause one stock's transfer to a specific redeemer to revert transfers that redeemer's value to the remaining holder set, which the attacker can be part of.

The behaviour is documented as intentional (`:198-202`) and tested (`test/vault/StockBasket.t.sol:482`), but the tradeoff is avoidable.

**Recommendation**: Do not burn value the user did not receive — credit the failed slice for later pull:

```solidity
mapping(address => mapping(address => uint256)) public unpaid; // token => user => owed

// in redeem's payout loop:
try this._payout(stocks[i], to, amounts[i]) {}
catch { unpaid[stocks[i]][to] += amounts[i]; emit PayoutDeferred(stocks[i], to, amounts[i]); amounts[i] = 0; }

function claimUnpaid(address token, address to) external nonReentrant returns (uint256 amt) {
    amt = unpaid[token][msg.sender];
    require(amt > 0, unicode"Nothing owed / 无欠款");
    unpaid[token][msg.sender] = 0;
    IERC20(token).safeTransfer(to, amt);
}
```

**Important**: deferred balances must be excluded from the pro-rata `balanceOf` reads in `previewRedeem`/`redeem` (`:217`, `:267`) — subtract a `totalDeferred[token]` accumulator — or the owed slice is double-counted as reserves.

### M-04: Unbounded-gas call to the untrusted Flap tax token can grief `distribute`

**Severity**: Medium · **Status**: Open
**File**: `src/vault/StockpileBasketVaultV2.sol:560-572`

**Description**: `_currentTaxBps()` calls `ITaxToken(t).taxRate()` (`:564`) with **no gas stipend**, inside `try/catch`. `taxToken` is the Flap-launched token, attacker-influenceable in the general case (Flap launches are permissionless; `newVault` accepts whatever `taxToken` the portal passes). The D18 guard at `:563` checks only for code presence, not behaviour.

```solidity
// keeper calls distributeUniform{gas: 10_000_000}(...)
// -> _currentTaxBps consumes ~9.84M, catch resumes with ~156k, _swapAll OOGs, whole tx reverts.
```

**Recommendation**: Cap the stipend — the getter needs a few thousand gas at most:

```solidity
try ITaxToken(t).taxRate{gas: 50_000}() returns (uint256 r) { ... } catch {}
```

Also latch on a successful call even when `r == 0`, via a separate `taxRateProbed` flag, so a legitimately zero-rate token is not re-probed every round.

```solidity
}
minted = StockBasket(_basket).deposit(amounts, spentWBNB, address(this));
for (uint256 i = 0; i < n; i++) {
    if (amounts[i] > 0) IERC20(_stocks[i].stock).forceApprove(_basket, 0);           // :648 unguarded
}
```

**Impact**: The reachable case is a token that permits `transfer` (so `swapLeg` succeeded and `amounts[i] > 0`) but reverts on `approve` — e.g. a compliance token gating `approve` by spender whitelist, or reverting when the *owner* is flagged. (`forceApprove` handles the USDT-style reset-to-zero case, so that is not the trigger; a hard revert is.) When it triggers, the entire `_distribute` reverts at `:642` — *after* the swaps executed in the same tx, so they roll back too. Every subsequent `distribute` hits the same leg identically. **The vault is fully bricked for distribution** while the condition holds, despite `_swapAll`, `StockBasket.deposit` and `_feedGrid` all being carefully designed to degrade gracefully. Recovery requires a Guardian sweep plus a beacon upgrade — there is no setter for `_stocks` after `setupMarket`.

**Recommendation**: Isolate the approvals like every other leg interaction, zeroing `amounts[i]` on failure so the basket does not attempt the pull:

```solidity
for (uint256 i = 0; i < n; i++) {
    if (amounts[i] == 0) continue;
    try this.approveStock(_stocks[i].stock, _basket, amounts[i]) {}
    catch { amounts[i] = 0; emit LegSkipped(i, _stocks[i].stock, 0); }
}

function approveStock(address token, address spender, uint256 amt) external {
    require(msg.sender == address(this), unicode"Only self / 仅限自身");
    IERC20(token).forceApprove(spender, amt);
}
```

Apply the same treatment to the reset loop at `:648`. **Must be paired with the H-02 fix**, otherwise a zeroed leg still mints full-value shares.

## Low

### L-01: `deadline` is caller-supplied and unvalidated

**Severity**: Low · **File**: `src/vault/StockpileBasketVaultV2.sol:450`, `:466`, `:624`

`deadline` flows straight into `ExactInputParams.deadline` with no bound. `type(uint256).max` disables the protection entirely, letting a keeper's transaction sit in the mempool and execute later at an arbitrarily worse price. Conversely a stale value makes the router revert on **every** leg, all caught at `:601` — a completely silent no-op that still consumes the `minInterval` window (M-01). A keeper automation with clock skew produces `Distributed(caller, gross, 0, net, 0, 0)` with nothing to signal the error.

**Recommendation**: `require(deadline >= block.timestamp && deadline <= block.timestamp + 1 hours, unicode"Bad deadline / 截止时间无效");` in `_distribute`.

### L-02: Skipped legs are unobservable

**Severity**: Low · **Status**: ✅ Fixed · **File**: `src/vault/StockpileBasketVaultV2.sol:601-603`, `:665-667`; `src/vault/StockBasket.sol:162-164`

All three `catch` blocks are empty with only a comment; `Distributed` (`:545`) reports aggregate `spentWBNB` but never which legs were skipped or why. An operator cannot tell from chain data whether a round under-spent because a pool is dead, because `minOut` was too tight, because a stock blacklisted the basket (H-02, silently diluting holders), or because the grid push failed. **This is what makes every higher-severity finding above likely to persist undetected in production** — the single highest-leverage code-quality fix.

**Recommendation**: `event LegSkipped(uint256 indexed legIndex, address stock, uint256 amountIn)` at `:601`; `event GridFeedFailed(bytes32 assetHash, uint256 amount)` at `:665`; `event PullSkipped(uint256 indexed i, address stock, uint256 amount)` in `StockBasket.deposit`. Capture the revert reason via `catch (bytes memory reason)` where cheap.

### L-03: Stock residue from skipped pulls is never re-deposited

**Severity**: Low · **Status**: ✅ Fixed · **File**: `src/vault/StockpileBasketVaultV2.sol:637-650`

`_depositToBasket` deposits exactly this round's `amounts[]`. Stock left on the vault by a previously-skipped pull (H-02) or a partial fill is never picked up later — it accumulates outside the basket, invisible to `previewRedeem`, recoverable only via a Guardian sweep, at which point it exits the seat-holder economy entirely.

**Recommendation**: Deposit balances, not deltas: `amounts[i] = IERC20(_stocks[i].stock).balanceOf(address(this))`. Combined with the H-02 fix, a transient failure then self-heals on the next round — exactly as `_feedGrid` already does for basket shares (`:661`).

### L-04: Zero-weight legs accepted; last leg absorbs all rounding dust

**Severity**: Low · **File**: `src/vault/StockpileBasketVaultV2.sol:370-382`, `:586-593`

`setupMarket` validates only `weightSum == 10_000` (`:382`); an individual `swapWeightBps == 0` passes. Such a leg gets `amountIn == 0` and is `continue`d forever (`:594`) — a permanent basket member that is never bought, yet still consuming a slot in every `redeem`/`previewRedeem`/`reserves` loop and misleading anyone reading `getStocks()`. Separately, `_swapAll` gives the final leg `net - allocated` (`:589`), so it absorbs every leg's truncation dust — and if the last leg has weight 0 it still receives that dust, contradicting its declared 0%.

**Recommendation**: `require(weights[i] > 0, unicode"Zero weight / 权重为零");` inside the loop at `:377`. Optionally route dust to the largest-weight leg rather than positionally.

## Info — verified positive properties

### I-01: Correct `receive()` shape, correct `_disableInitializers`, verified `CreateGridParams` ABI

`receive()` is an empty body (`:411`) — no loops, no external calls, no delegatecall, trivially under the 1M-gas rule. The constructor calls `_disableInitializers()` (`:286`), so the implementation behind the beacon cannot be initialized by anyone; the impl was additionally verified inert against direct calls (`setupMarket()` reverts in `abi.decode` of an empty `_stocksBlob`; `registerWithGrid`/`distribute` revert on `basket == address(0)`). The vault's local `CreateGridParams` (`:26-33`) was diffed field-by-field against canonical `GridTypes.CreateGridParams` (`src/libraries/GridTypes.sol:44-51`) — `uint32,uint16,uint32,address,address,uint128`, **exact match**, so the `createGrid` ABI encoding is correct.

### I-02: Share-minting design is immune to donation / first-depositor inflation

Shares are minted as an absolute WBNB-value quantity (`sharesToMint = spentWBNB`), never as a function of current reserves. This structurally eliminates the ERC-4626-style first-depositor inflation attack and makes direct token donations to the basket strictly beneficial to all holders. Redemption reads live `balanceOf` rather than a cached `reserves` variable (`:217`) — correct-by-construction for fee-on-transfer stocks and immune to accounting drift. The FoT path was traced end-to-end (`swapLeg` measures a balance delta at `:629`; the basket pays out only what it holds) with no over-withdrawal path found. **Note this property is undermined by H-02**, which breaks the value-in anchor.

### I-03: No reentrancy path across BNB → WBNB → swap → mint → grid-push

Every external call reachable from `distribute` was traced. Both entrypoints are `nonReentrant`; `StockBasket.deposit`/`redeem` share the basket's own guard, so a hostile stock re-entering `redeem` from a `transferFrom` hook is caught and skipped rather than double-redeeming. `swapLeg`, `_pull` and `_payout` are all `require(msg.sender == address(this))`-gated and unreachable externally (tested at `test/vault/StockpileBasketVaultV2.t.sol:739`, `:746`). `_currentTaxBps`'s call to the untrusted tax token compiles to `STATICCALL` because the interface declares `view`, so it cannot mutate state at all. Two external calls precede the `lastDistribute` effect (WBNB `deposit{value:}` at `:507`, the `taxRate()` staticcall at `:564`) — a CEI deviation, but not exploitable given the guard. See M-04 for the gas-griefing angle that *does* apply.

### I-04: `assetHash` domain separation blocks adapter squatting

Whether a third party could front-run `registerWithGrid()` and hijack the vault's `assetHash` — which would make `receiveYieldERC20` fail its `msg.sender == assetAdapter[assetHash]` check forever and silently strand every grid push — was investigated and found **unreachable**: `registerAsset` requires `approvedAdapters[msg.sender]` (`UnifiedGridManager.sol:594`), a Guardian-only allowlist, and the hash is `keccak256(abi.encode("StockpileBasketVaultV2", address(this), gid))`, domain-separated by both a type string and the vault address, so sibling adapters cannot collide with it. Residual risk noted for completeness: registration is permanent-on-first-write and `withdrawAsset` is callable only by the registering adapter, so a *compromised approved adapter* could permanently block one vault — a Guardian-gated precondition with no user-reachable attack.

## Centralization Analysis

### Admin controls

There is exactly one privileged principal on the vault: the Flap chain **Guardian**, hardcoded in `VaultBase._getGuardian()` (`src/vault/flap/VaultBase.sol:85-98`) and therefore genuinely irrevocable, as Rule 001 requires. It controls `setKeeper` (`:687`), `setCommissionBps` (`:695`), `setTreasury` (`:702`), `setMinInterval` (`:709`), `emergencyWithdrawToken` (`:717`), `emergencyWithdrawNative` (`:727`), plus `upgradeVaultImplementation` and `lockVaultUpgrades` on the factory.

There is **no owner, no admin role, no creator role** — the token launcher has zero post-launch authority. That is a strong fairness property, but it creates a hard **liveness dependency**: `initialize` sets no keepers, so the vault cannot distribute at all until the Guardian appoints one or calls `distribute` itself. Guardian inaction means the Flap tax stream accrues as idle BNB indefinitely.

Two controls are notably well-designed. `setCommissionBps` is **monotonically decreasing** (`require(newBps <= commissionBps)`, `:696`) and hard-capped at `MAX_COMMISSION_BPS = 1000`, so seat holders provably keep ≥90% of every receipt and the Guardian can never raise the take — a genuine, verifiable user protection. `setTreasury` is unbounded, but since the commission is capped, redirecting it is bounded-loss.

The unbounded lever is `emergencyWithdrawToken`, which drains the full balance of any token — WBNB awaiting distribution, purchased stocks, minted basket shares not yet pushed to the grid. It is correctly `onlyGuardian` (not `onlyOwnerOrGuardian`) and `nonReentrant`, and Rule 009 mandates its existence, but it means the Guardian can unilaterally capture an entire round's value at any moment. Users must trust the Flap Guardian completely.

### Upgrade mechanisms

All vaults share one `UpgradeableBeacon` deployed in the factory constructor (`Factory:104`). Because OZ v4's `UpgradeableBeacon(address)` sets `owner = msg.sender`, the beacon is owned by the **factory contract**, whose only upgrade entrypoint is Guardian-gated. No other path to `upgradeTo` exists: no owner, no `transferOwnership` exposure, no timelock bypass. Upgrade authority is therefore genuinely Guardian-only and correctly satisfies the proxy-vault variant of Rule 009.

The tradeoff: one upgrade silently rewrites **every** vault the factory ever produced, with no timelock and no per-vault opt-out. `lockVaultUpgrades()` renounces beacon ownership permanently — note that calling it also **permanently forecloses the only recovery path for H-01**.

### Emergency functions

Both emergency withdrawals match the Rule 009 shape verbatim: `onlyGuardian`, `nonReentrant`, full-balance drain, caller-supplied `address to`, correct events, no `amount` parameter, no hardcoded destination. No auto-forward mechanism exists, so the `autoForwardEnabled` default-false requirement is moot.

### Decentralization recommendations

1. **Timelock `upgradeVaultImplementation`** (48–72h) with an `UpgradeQueued` event, so holders can exit seats before an implementation change lands. Today an upgrade is atomic and unannounced across all vaults simultaneously.
2. **Give the launcher a bounded, non-custodial role**: the ability to add/remove keepers for *their own* vault. A launcher whose Guardian never appoints a keeper currently has a permanently inert vault and no recourse. Guardian must retain override per Rule 001.
3. **Make `distribute` permissionless once slippage is oracle-derived** (H-03). Keeper-gating exists solely because `minOut` is caller-trusted; with an on-chain TWAP floor the gate becomes unnecessary and the Guardian liveness dependency disappears entirely.
4. Consider a **two-step withdraw with a short delay** for non-crisis amounts, reserving the instant path for a Guardian-declared emergency flag.
5. **Fix H-01 with a permissionless `claimGridPayout`**, not a Guardian-gated one, so grid-revenue recovery does not add yet another Guardian dependency.

## Best Practices and Code Quality

### Positive observations

- **NatSpec quality is exceptional and, importantly, *accurate*** where spot-checked against behaviour — the D18 codeless-token rationale (`:107-112`), the split-init gas rationale (`:100-105`), and the commission-proportionality argument (`:486-492`) all match the code. The commission math was verified by hand across two rounds with a skipped leg, confirming the claim that retained WBNB is taxed exactly once: round 1 (gross 100, one 10% leg skipped) skims 5.4 and retains 10.0; round 2 skims 0.6 on that 10.0 — total 6.0, exactly one 6% charge on the original 100.
- Bilingual literal `require` strings throughout; **zero custom errors** in project code (Rule 004).
- The self-call isolation pattern (`swapLeg`, `_pull`, `_payout`) is a clean, correctly-gated way to get per-leg atomicity, and each is tested for its `Only self` guard.
- Deliberate, documented FoT handling via balance deltas rather than trusting router return values (`:629`).
- The off-vault `StockBasketDeployer` (EIP-170 relief) is sound minimal-privilege design: stateless, permissionless, retains nothing.
- `StockBasket.renounceOwnership` is explicitly disabled (`:119-121`), closing the strand-before-`setVault` hole.
- Broad test coverage: 92 vault-suite tests including a `receive()` gas test, D18 codeless-token coverage, commission cases at 4 tax rates, and best-effort skip paths.

### Areas for improvement

- **Silent failure is the dominant anti-pattern.** Five `catch` blocks swallow errors with no event (L-02). The resilience story depends on skips being observed off-chain, but nothing is emitted to observe.
- **Best-effort discipline is applied inconsistently**: swaps, pulls and the grid push are isolated, but the `forceApprove` calls sitting between them are not (M-05). Either everything on the leg path is isolated or the guarantee is illusory.
- The `__gap` comment at `:221-223` claims the layout targets "a round 50-slot budget"; the contract declares 12 slots + 43 gap = 55. Harmless, but correct it before someone shrinks `__gap` based on it during an upgrade.
- Every basket is deployed with the identical hardcoded name/symbol `"Stockpile Basket Share"` / `"SPBSK"` (`:387`). With one basket per launched token, wallets and explorers will show many indistinguishable SPBSK tokens. Derive from the tax-token symbol or the grid id.
- `_stocks` is immutable after `setupMarket` with no setter. Given a leg can become permanently toxic (H-02, M-05), a Guardian-only `replaceLeg`/`disableLeg` would avoid forcing a full beacon upgrade over one bad token.
- `IUGM` (`:39-47`) declares only three of the UGM functions the vault needs; `claimPayout` is missing entirely — the interface-level symptom of H-01.
- `MIN_TOTAL_SEATS`/`MAX_SINGLE_TX_CREATE_TOTAL_SEATS`/`MIN_TAX_BPS`/`MAX_TAX_BPS` are duplicated as literals in the vault (`:127-131`), mirroring the UGM's own constants. If the UGM's bounds change, the vault silently accepts configs that `createGrid` will reject at `setupMarket` time. Read them from the UGM at init, or add a deploy-time assertion.

## Testing and Verification Recommendations

The suite is strong on happy paths and input validation but has one systematic blind spot: **all vault tests use `MockUGM`**, which models neither creator payouts, nor seat sales, nor Harberger tax. That is precisely why H-01 is invisible today.

1. **H-01 — integration test against the real `UnifiedGridManager`.** Deploy the real UGM, run `setupMarket`, have accounts `buySeat` the primary listing, assert `ugm.payouts(WBNB, address(vault)) > 0`, and prove no vault selector retrieves it. Add a regression test that `claimGridPayout` recovers exactly that balance, and a second asserting claimed *basket* shares are not re-pushed into the grid.
2. **H-02 — dilution regression.** Two rounds: round 1 all legs healthy, snapshot `previewRedeem(1e18)`; round 2 with one stock's `transferFrom` reverting; assert per-share backing is **unchanged** (post-fix) and `totalSupply()` grew only by the value actually landed. **`test/vault/StockBasket.t.sol:559` currently asserts the buggy behaviour and must be inverted.**
3. **H-03 — fork-based sandwich test.** On a BSC fork with real Pancake V3 pools, front-run `distributeUniform` with a large swap in one leg's pool, execute, back-run, and assert attacker profit is bounded by the oracle floor. Plus a unit test that a uniform `minOut` sized for the highest-priced leg does **not** silently skip the others.
4. **M-01 — interval accounting.** Assert `lastDistribute` is **unchanged** after an all-skipped round (stale deadline, and separately unreachable `minOut`), and that an immediate follow-up `distribute` succeeds.
5. **M-02 — gas ceiling.** `testSetupMarketGasAtMaxGridSize`: `gridSize = MAX_SINGLE_TX_CREATE_TOTAL_SEATS` against the **real** UGM, `assertLt(gasUsed, 30_000_000)`. Fails today at ~64M; passes after the cap is lowered.
6. **M-03 — redemption forfeiture.** Assert a redeemer whose payout leg reverts retains a claim (post-fix `unpaid` balance), and that `previewRedeem` does not double-count deferred balances as reserves.
7. **M-04 — gas-bomb tax token.** Mock a `taxRate()` with an unbounded loop; assert `distribute` still completes within a 10M-gas budget once the stipend cap is added.
8. **M-05 — approve-reverting stock.** Mock a stock that permits `transfer` but reverts `approve`; assert `distribute` completes and skips only that leg.
9. **FoT / tax-token legs end-to-end.** `MockFeeOnTransferERC20` exists but no test configures a Flap **tax token as a stock leg** — the realistic case where `transferFrom` debits more than is credited. Assert no over-withdrawal and correct share sizing.
10. **Schema↔ABI drift test** (Rule 006 gap #2): loop over `schema.methods`, `abi.encodeWithSignature` each, `staticcall` the reads, assert no empty returndata. Would have caught V-01.
11. **`receive()` gas under adversarial state.** Extend `testReceiveGasUnder1M` to run post-`setupMarket` with 20 legs configured and a non-empty grid.
12. **Beacon upgrade storage-layout test.** Populate every storage variable, upgrade to a V3 that appends a field and shrinks `__gap` by one, assert every prior value survives. This is the guard that makes the H-01 recovery path trustworthy.
13. **20-leg gas envelope.** `distribute` at `MAX_STOCKS = 20` against real V3 routes, asserting total gas stays within a single-tx budget.
14. **Fix the default RPC** in `test/vault/FlapBSCFixture.sol:241` so `forge test` is green out of the box.

## Conclusion

`StockpileBasketVaultV2` passes the Flap V2 spec on every Critical and High rule: unmodified `VaultBaseV2`, a 1,147-gas `receive()`, literal bilingual error strings, the exact Rule 002 commission formula under a decreasing 10% cap, complete Rule 006 coverage, and provably Guardian-only upgrade authority with zero launcher privileges. The single spec FAIL (V-01, incomplete `vaultUISchema`) is a UI-completeness issue with a mechanical fix.

The findings that should block deployment are in value routing, not conformance. **H-01** silently strands the grid's entire creator-side revenue — the primary economic output of the Harberger design — and becomes unrecoverable the moment `lockVaultUpgrades()` is called. **H-02** breaks the basket's core "one share = fixed value in" invariant whenever a leg misbehaves, and is currently enshrined as correct by the test suite. **H-03** leaves the money path without meaningful slippage protection under any operationally viable configuration.

Recommended order: fix H-01 (add `claimGridPayout` + the missing `IUGM` declaration), H-02 (size the mint to value landed), and L-02 (emit on every skip — cheap, and it makes every other failure mode observable) before mainnet. H-03 needs an oracle-derived floor, which is a larger change and can follow, provided keeper appointment stays strictly Guardian-controlled in the interim. Fix the fixture RPC default before submitting to Flap so the review runs green.

**Post-audit update.** H-01, H-02, M-01, M-05, L-02 and L-03 have been fixed in this repo; the full suite is green at 223/223 including both real-mainnet fork tests. See *Remediation Status* near the top for the change list and each finding's *Remediation applied* note for detail. H-03 was consciously **accepted rather than fixed**, with keeper-gating as the stated control and the residual risk documented in that finding — a reviewer should treat it as an open trust assumption on the Flap Guardian, not as a closed issue. The remaining open findings (V-01, V-02, M-02, M-03, M-04, L-01, L-04) are unchanged, and the `FlapBSCFixture` RPC default still needs fixing before submission.
